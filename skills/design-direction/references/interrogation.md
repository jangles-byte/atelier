# Interrogating the Brief

Five questions, answered in writing, before any visual decision. Each has a default
inference strategy for when the user hasn't said — infer and state your assumption
rather than blocking.

## 1. Purpose — what must this artifact *do*?

Not "look nice." Every visual artifact has a job: convert a visitor, make a number
legible at a glance, make a game feel responsive, make an audience remember one claim.
Write the job in one sentence. Every later trade-off resolves toward the job.

*Infer from:* the artifact type. Dashboards → fast comprehension of state. Landing
pages → one action taken. Slides → one idea remembered per slide. Games → feel.

## 2. Audience — who looks at this, and in what state?

A trader glancing between windows, a commuter on a phone in sunlight, an executive
being pitched, a player mid-combat. The viewer's context sets information density,
minimum type sizes, contrast requirements, and how much motion is welcome.

*Infer from:* domain. When genuinely unknown, design for the least patient plausible
viewer.

## 3. Tone — three adjectives and three anti-adjectives

Name three words the design should evoke (**precise, warm, confident**) and three it
must not (**not playful, not corporate, not dense**). The anti-adjectives are the more
useful half: they are what you check drafts against. A design that could be described
by one of its anti-adjectives has failed the brief.

*Infer from:* the product's voice, the user's language, the domain's expectations —
then push one notch away from the domain default (see question 5).

## 4. Constraints — what is fixed?

Existing brand colors, a required framework, accessibility floor (WCAG AA is the
default floor, always), platform conventions, performance budget, deadline. List them
so the philosophy is written inside reality. Constraints are generative: the best
signature moves usually come from an aggressive reading of a constraint.

## 5. Differentiation — what does the default look like, and where do we deviate?

Picture the template version of this artifact — the thing a site builder would emit.
Name two or three of its features ("hero with centered headline, three feature cards,
purple gradient"). Then choose **exactly where** this design deviates. Deviating
nowhere is genericism; deviating everywhere is chaos. One to three deliberate
deviations, one of which becomes the signature move.

## Output

Condense the five answers into the philosophy document
(see `philosophy-format.md`). Total interrogation output: under 150 words. If it takes
longer, the answers aren't sharp yet.
